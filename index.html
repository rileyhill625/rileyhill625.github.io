End-to-End Greenfield Grid Design — West Texas

A clean-sheet design of the grid to serve a new large load in the West Texas / Permian Basin corridor — the hottest load-growth problem in U.S. power, and in-state. A greenfield load-serving substation fed from a new 345 kV transmission tap, a co-located solar + wind + battery plant, the complete protection scheme (transmission-line, transformer-differential, bus, breaker-failure, feeder, and inverter-based-resource protection), and a SCADA/EMS layer with operator HMIs. The flagship that folds every project on this site — protection, analysis, comms, and renewables — into one working grid, designed end to end by a single engineer.

About

Why power, and why both.

I'm an electrical engineering senior at Texas A&M passionate about many aspects of the electrical field, but most prominently protection and control and renewables integration with the electrical grid. I find this area of electrical engineering to be one of the most important and interesting areas of the profession, and practically the entire nation relies on it. Understanding the physics of how a digital relay keeps an entire neighborhood from losing power on a regular basis motivates me to pursue a career in this field.

Most of the work done on this website is self directed. They're projects I scoped and built out to understand how the grid is actually protected and how renewables change that picture, with online resources and ai assisted learning. I think it's important to understand both the renewables side of grid design as well as the protection & control side in order to be a well-rounded power engineer capable of working in this dynamic field. From coordination studies to the communication layer between substations, to pv interconnection, it all ties into a proper understanding of the electrical grid. I passed the FE in April 2026 and I'm aiming for a full-time P&C role after I graduate in May 2027.
